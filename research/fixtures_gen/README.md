# Fixture generator

Non-shipping research code that writes the procedural part of [`fixtures/`](../../fixtures/README.md): the clean base meshes, one model per defect class, and reference seam sets. Every file it writes is committed, and the tests prove the committed files are exactly what it produces.

```sh
python3 generate.py            # rewrite fixtures/meshes (including meshes/source-seams and the symmetry, faceted and quadruped models), fixtures/defects, fixtures/seams
python3 -m unittest -v         # oracles over the committed files, plus a byte-for-byte regeneration check
```

Standard library only (`requirements.txt` is empty on purpose). The interpreters it was run with are in [`results.md`](results.md). Coordinates are rounded to 6 decimals, so a last-bit difference in a platform's `sin`/`cos` would only show up if it straddled a rounding tie [solid: the nearest current value is 3e-8 mm from one, about a million times a last-bit error at this scale].

## What it makes

All coordinates are millimetres, faces are triangles wound counter-clockwise seen from outside, and OBJ indices are 1-based.

| File | Shape |
|---|---|
| `meshes/cube.obj` | 90 mm cube, each side a 3 × 3 grid of quads (108 triangles). A 12-triangle cube has no interior vertices, so a chart would have nothing to flatten and the defects would have to sit on top of each other; the grid gives each face chart 4 interior vertices and keeps every coordinate and edge midpoint exact |
| `meshes/sphere.obj` | Radius 50 mm, 12 segments × 8 bands (168 triangles) |
| `meshes/capsule.obj` | Radius 30 mm, 120 mm long: two hemispheres and a cylinder (192 triangles) |
| `defects/*.obj` | The cube plus exactly one defect each; the second header line says what and where |
| `seams/*.json` | One reference seam set per mesh: sphere in six gores, capsule as two caps and a slit tube, cube as a cross-shaped net, in the [seam-set format](../../fixtures/README.md#seam-set-format) |
| `meshes/source-seams/capsule-uv.obj`, `.glb` | The capsule with UV coordinates cut along meridian 0 (pole to pole) and around the tube's upper rim (z = +30 mm, where it meets the hemisphere), so two UV islands: the upper hemisphere and the rest. Written by `source_seams.py` the way Blender's exporters write a UV-unwrapped model: the glTF has one vertex per distinct (position, UV), so it splits at every cut, and flips V; the OBJ indexes a `vt` per corner. Pole corners in an island share one UV, so a pole fan carries no cut. `seams/capsule-uv.json` lists the cuts by the OBJ's vertex indices |
| `meshes/source-seams/capsule-hardshade.glb` | The same capsule and UVs, with the lower hemisphere flat-shaded, so its glTF also splits vertices where normals differ. Its UV cuts are still exactly `seams/capsule-uv.json` |
| `meshes/source-seams/sphere-band.obj`, `.glb` | The sphere with UVs cut around two rings (z = ±35.36 mm), like a neck and a waist: two caps and the band between them, each its own UV island in a polar projection, so no meridian is cut. The band is an annulus, so it cannot lie flat in one piece. Its seam set is `seams/non-disk/sphere-band.json`, kept out of `seams/`' top level because the every-chart-is-a-disk check covers only that level |
| `meshes/source-seams/three-materials.obj`, `.glb` | The capsule in three materials, no UVs: `minky / cuddle` on the upper hemisphere, `Polar fleece ` (trailing space kept) on the tube, `Felt Nose` on the lower hemisphere. The OBJ has one `o` and switches `usemtl` twice inside it; the glTF has one primitive per material, each with its own vertices, as Blender exports it. After trimming and lower-casing, the first two names equal 001's `Minky / cuddle` and `Polar fleece` presets and the third equals none. `seams/non-disk/three-materials.json` lists the material boundaries (both tube rims) as `fabricBoundary` |
| `meshes/source-seams/dead-end.obj`, `.glb` | Written by `source_dead_end.py`. The capsule without its bottom pole fan (an opening), with a `Head` upper hemisphere and a `Body` rest, so the tube's upper rim is a material boundary. Cut A runs from that boundary to the opening; D1 runs down from the boundary and stops after two edges; D2 leaves A along the tube's lower rim and stops after two edges. UVs are a planar projection offset per corner group (corners joined across an uncut edge share one), so exactly the cut edges read as cuts. `seams/non-disk/dead-end.json` lists the cuts and `seams/non-disk/dead-end.tips.json` the two dead-end tips |
| `meshes/source-seams/no-uv.obj` | The plain capsule (the same `v` and `f` lines as `meshes/capsule.obj`) as one object in one material, `Body`, with no `vt` lines |
| `meshes/source-seams/reshaped-v1.glb`, `reshaped-v2.glb` | Written by `source_reshaped.py`. capsule-uv's mesh and UV cuts with a `Head` upper hemisphere and a `Body` rest, so the tube's upper rim is both a UV cut and a material boundary. v2 stretches the lower cap 1.5× deeper below the tube's lower rim and moves nothing else, so 18 of the 21 seams re-place on v2 by position and the 3 touching the cap cannot |
| `meshes/near-symmetric.glb`, `asymmetric.glb` | Written by `source_symmetry.py`. Two heads, each a radial graph over a 32 × 16 latitude-longitude grid (482 vertices, 960 triangles): an ellipsoid (semi-axes 40, 55, 45 mm) raised by smooth, compactly supported bumps. `near-symmetric` has a snout and two ears and is mirror-symmetric about x = 0, bit for bit, except that its +x ear leans 5° forward. `asymmetric` has its snout turned aside, one ear and two broad lumps, so no plane mirrors it. GLB only, POSITION and indices, for 002's symmetry detector (SC-005) |
| `meshes/quadruped.glb` | Written by `source_quadruped.py`. A symmetric four-legged animal, 234 × 73 × 159 mm (4,784 vertices, 9,564 triangles): an ellipsoid body and belly, capsule neck, sphere head and capsule muzzle joined hard, so the neck and the muzzle fold are true concave creases, with two ears, four legs and a tail blended in smoothly (log-sum-exp, 4 mm), so no other crease exists. Meshed by dual contouring on a 4 mm grid symmetric about x = 0, the crease solve used only in cells whose crossing normals disagree by more than 25°; every cell at x < 0 mirrors its twin, so the model is mirror-symmetric bit for bit. GLB only, POSITION and indices. 002's primary tuning fixture (T001): its neck and muzzle creases, head dome and flat flanks are what every default threshold is judged against |
| `meshes/faceted.glb` | Written by `source_faceted.py`. A frequency-2 icosphere, radius 60 mm (42 vertices, 80 triangles): every one of its 120 edges folds 18–22.5° and is 33–37 mm long, so a crease detector sees more hard edges than the 50-hint cap and no chain-length filter can call them noise. A plain icosahedron has only 30 edges, under the cap. GLB only, POSITION and indices, for 002's hint-flood path (SC-010) |

### Defect fixtures

Each one but `non-orientable` is the cube with one change, chosen so each fixture trips exactly its own detector on its input mesh, or exactly those the table below names when one change cannot help tripping more. The oracle welds coincident positions and drops duplicate faces, then evaluates every topology detector together on that welded, de-duplicated mesh; it repairs nothing.

| Class | Change | Why it does not trip anything else |
|---|---|---|
| `non-manifold-edge` | A closed tetrahedron shares one cube edge (4 faces on it) | Its faces join the cube's fan at both edge ends, so no bowtie; winding is judged only across 2-face edges |
| `bowtie-vertex` | A closed tetrahedron touches one cube corner | Components are counted by shared vertices, so on input it is still one component |
| `flipped-normals` | The +x side is wound inward | Still closed and manifold; the signed volume stays positive |
| `zero-area-face` | One top face is split at the midpoint of edge `ab`, and a collinear sliver `(a, b, midpoint)` closes the gap | Still closed, manifold and consistently wound |
| `duplicate-face` | One face repeated, same winding | The extra edge use disappears once duplicates are dropped |
| `opposite-duplicate-face` | One face repeated, wound the other way | Not only its own class in the engine: it keeps a back-to-back pair as a double-sided sheet, so each of the face's three edges has three faces (non-manifold). The oracle, which drops duplicates by vertex set, sees only the duplicate |
| `repeated-index-face` | An extra face naming the first face's first vertex twice | It has no area either, so both oracles also count it zero-area. Both keep it out of the topology, so it makes no hole or non-manifold edge |
| `hole` | The centre quad of the top side removed | One 4-edge boundary loop, no bowtie |
| `unwelded-split-vertices` | The top side gets its own coincident copies of its 12 border vertices, as a glTF exporter splits at a UV seam | Welding by position restores the clean cube |
| `self-intersection` | One top vertex pushed through the bottom side, off the grid lines | Topology unchanged; the crossings are transverse |
| `multi-component` | A separate closed tetrahedron beside the cube | Closed, manifold, outward |
| `non-orientable` | Not the cube: a Möbius strip of 12 quads, 40 mm radius and 20 mm wide, with one half twist | It cannot trip only its own detector. A closed surface in 3D can be non-orientable only by passing through itself, so the strip has an open edge (boundary), and with no winding that fits every face, two faces disagree across the edge where the twist closes (winding) |

"Exactly one" holds for the input, not after repair. Repair splits bowtie vertices before it counts components, so `bowtie-vertex` becomes two components, the cube and the tetrahedron, and a repaired run also reports `multi-component` [solid]. The `non-manifold-edge` fin can likewise end up as its own component once its edge is split [likely]. A test over the engine's repair report should assert that the named class is present, not that it is the only one.

Non-finite coordinates (NaN, ∞) are not a defect class here: the engine rejects them at import with `EngineError::NonFiniteInput` and never repairs them, so they belong with the OBJ import tests rather than in the corpus that measures detection.

## The oracles

`test_fixtures.py`, `test_source_seams.py`, `test_source_materials.py`, `test_dead_end.py` and `test_reshaped.py` read the committed files and share no code with `generate.py`, `source_seams.py`, `source_dead_end.py` or `source_reshaped.py`, except the one test that regenerates into a temporary directory and compares bytes:

- **Clean meshes**: every detector reads zero, Euler characteristic 2, no unused vertex, positive signed volume.
- **Defects**: one file per class and no others; each file trips exactly its own detector, or exactly the list the table above gives, with a separate check that no flipping of `non-orientable`'s faces makes every shared edge agree while one does for `flipped-normals`. Detectors: coincident positions (weld), faces naming a vertex twice (kept out of the topology, and zero-area), repeated vertex sets, faces per edge (> 2 non-manifold, 1 boundary), repeated directed edges across 2-face edges plus closed components with signed volume ≤ 0 (winding), face fans per vertex joined through shared edges (bowtie), vertex-connected components, area below `1e-12 × bbox diagonal²`, and Moller–Trumbore edge-through-triangle tests between faces that share no vertex (self-intersection).
- **Seams**: the JSON is type-checked field by field (an index of `1.0` or `false` is rejected, not compared equal); every listed pair is a mesh edge; cutting virtually (faces flood-filled across uncut edges, corners joined across uncut edges) leaves every chart with Euler characteristic 1 and one boundary loop. The same oracle must reject an uncut closed mesh and an annulus, so it cannot pass vacuously.
- **Source seams**: the UV cuts recomputed from the OBJ's `vt` (an interior edge whose corner UVs differ across it at either endpoint) are exactly the reference seam set, and the rule is checked on a cut that differs at one endpoint only. The layout itself is pinned: 9 meridian edges pole to pole plus the 12 edges of the tube's upper rim, leaving two disk-shaped charts. The glTF is a GLB with valid chunks; matched to the OBJ by position, it has the same triangles and the same corner UVs (V flipped), with one vertex per distinct (position, UV) and no more. Its POSITION bounds equal the data. `capsule-hardshade.glb`, matched to the same OBJ, has the same triangles and UV cuts, more than 50 interior edges whose normals differ but whose UVs do not, and unit-length normals. `sphere-band`'s UV cuts equal its seam set, are two full 12-edge rings at mirrored heights, and leave exactly two disks and one annulus. Every face keeps its outside-seen counter-clockwise winding in UV space, so no island is mirrored.
- **Materials** (`test_source_materials.py`): the OBJ has one object and three `usemtl` runs with the exact names; the boundaries recomputed from those runs equal the seam set, are both tube rims, and leave two disks and a tube; two names match a preset only after trimming and lower-casing, and one matches none; the glTF has the same three names, one primitive per material in OBJ order with the run's triangles, no `TEXCOORD_0`, one vertex per position per primitive, and exact POSITION bounds.
- **No UVs**: `no-uv.obj` has one object, one material, no UVs, and exactly the capsule's positions and triangles.
- **Dead ends** (`test_dead_end.py`): the UV cuts equal the seam set; the opening is one 12-edge loop and the material boundary the tube's upper rim; the tips of the graph over cuts and material boundaries, excluding opening vertices, are exactly the two recorded; ignoring material boundaries adds two tips and ignoring the opening adds one, so both ways a cut may end are exercised; the glTF has the OBJ's two runs as primitives, with their triangles and UVs.
- **Symmetry** (`test_symmetry.py`): both models pass the clean-mesh detectors. The mirror score of a plane is the RMS distance from each vertex's reflection to the surface (closest point on a triangle), over the bbox diagonal. `near-symmetric`: every vertex mirrors bit-exactly onto a vertex except under 10%, on both ears and nowhere else; its score about x = 0 lies between 0.0005 and 0.005 (0.0035 as committed); a Fibonacci sweep of 60 normals with a bounded pattern search finds that plane from scratch. `asymmetric`: the same search finds no plane scoring under 0.01 (best 0.015), so the search that finds the near-symmetric plane cannot find one here.
- **Quadruped** (`test_quadruped.py`): closed, manifold, consistently wound outward, no bowtie, no coincident or unused vertex, no face under the corpus's area floor, and no self-intersection (the face-pair search runs through a spatial hash, since the corpus oracle's pairwise search is quadratic). Every vertex mirrors onto a vertex in x = 0. Animal-sized and quadruped-shaped: 200–260 mm long, 60–80 wide, 140–170 tall, a foot in each quadrant below z = 6 mm, a raised tail. Concave folds of at least 40° form rings around the neck (more than 20 edges) and the muzzle (more than 12), and appear nowhere else but the limb, ear and tail roots. The angle defect summed over the head dome exceeds 60° and more than three times the body flank's.
- **Faceted** (`test_faceted.py`): clean under every detector, 42 vertices and 80 triangles on the 60 mm sphere; all 120 interior edges fold more than 15° and are longer than 30 mm, more than the 50-hint cap; the fold measure reads zero on a flat pair, so it cannot pass vacuously.
- **Re-import** (`test_reshaped.py`): v1's UV cuts, welded by position across its two primitives, are exactly capsule-uv's seam set, and a face is `Head` exactly when all its corners lie at or above the upper rim; v2 has the same faces, materials and UVs, every position at or above the lower rim bit-identical and every one below it moved straight down; by position, exactly 18 of v1's 21 cuts re-place on v2, the other 3 all touch the cap, and the re-placed ones are still cuts in v2.
